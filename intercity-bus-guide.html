① Express vs. Intercity — the Biggest Trap Is "the Apps Are Completely Different"

In Korea, "the bus terminal" isn't one unified system. Express buses and intercity buses are separate legally, in booking apps, and in operating companies. There's no single app the way KTX has Korail Talk — download the wrong app without knowing this and you'll run straight into "the route I want doesn't show up in the search."

Category Express Bus (고속버스) Intercity Bus (시외버스)
Mainly connects Direct routes between major cities (Seoul↔Busan, Daejeon, Gwangju, etc.) Small and mid-size cities (including township/village-level stops)
Booking app/site kobus.co.kr · GosokBus T-money app Bustago (bustago.or.kr) · TxBus app
Stopping style Close to non-stop Passes through multiple intermediate stops (check ahead whether your bus actually stops there)
Seat classes All 3 classes available: standard, premium, first Mostly standard and premium (first class is rare)

The key point: if you search your destination and nothing shows up, that doesn't mean "there's no route" — it's a signal that you need to open a different app. Search kobus first if you're headed to a major city, Bustago first if it's a small city or a stop along the way.

Method Where Foreign Cards Notes
Official website kobus.co.kr / bustago.or.kr — supports English, Japanese, Chinese Some rejection rate (a VAN infrastructure quirk, see #527) Good for checking routes and schedules first
App GosokBus T-money / TxBus Can also get rejected Mostly Korean UI — even installed ahead of time, the screens themselves may feel unfamiliar
Terminal ticket window Each terminal's ticket window Cash and most cards OK The most reliable route — even if you can't say the destination out loud, showing it works
Kiosk Unmanned kiosks in the terminal lobby Varies by machine See the screen flow in ③

When foreign cards keep getting rejected on the website or app: search your route on Klook and pay there first (the approval rate for foreign cards is far higher). After booking is confirmed, you get a QR voucher, and on many routes you have to exchange this QR for a physical ticket at the terminal window or kiosk — some routes let you board directly with just the QR screen, so check your confirmation text or email for an "exchange required" notice.

③ Kiosk Screen Flow — Exactly as Shown

Unmanned kiosks generally follow this order.

1. Select 「Gosokbeoseu / Siwebeoseu (고속버스 / 시외버스) — Express Bus / Intercity Bus」 — this is where it forks, pick wrong and your route won't show up

2. 「Chulbalji → Dochakji (출발지 → 도착지) — Departure → Arrival」 search screen — type the destination in Korean or English

3. Select 「Nalja · sigan (날짜·시간) — Date/Time」 → touch the departure you want from the timetable list

4. 「Jwaseok deunggeup (좌석 등급) — Seat Class」 — choose among Standard (일반) Premium (우등) First (프리미엄)

5. On the 「Jwaseok baechido (좌석 배치도) — Seat Map」, touch your seat directly

6. 「Gyeolje sudan (결제 수단) — Payment Method」 → insert a card or feed in cash

7. Ticketing → prints out a ticket (or a QR printout); some machines print the boarding bay number on it too

If you're stuck on an options screen, it's usually because you missed the Confirm (확인) or Next (다음) button at the bottom of the screen.

④ On-Site Terminal Flow — From Ticket in Hand to Seated on the Bus

1. Enter the waiting room → check your bus on the departure board: the boarding bay (platform) number appears alongside the bus number (or destination and departure time)

2. Head to that boarding bay 5–10 minutes before departure — going early is fine, but since a bus sometimes arrives late at its assigned bay, check the departure board once more

3. Present your ticket or QR directly to the waiting driver at the boarding bay

4. The driver loads large luggage into the luggage bay on the side of the bus — if you're given a luggage tag (sticker), hold onto it, since it's checked against your bag when you get off

The fact that the boarding bay number often isn't revealed at the booking stage mirrors KTX (#1287, revealed 15 minutes before departure). If you can't find the departure board, the area near the central information desk in the waiting room is usually the most visible spot.

⑤ I Booked Online — So Why Do I Still Have to Print a Ticket at the Window?

For express buses (kobus, T-money), the booking confirmation screen (mobile QR) often works as the ticket itself. But for intercity buses (Bustago, TxBus), only a booking number gets issued, and on quite a few routes the driver only accepts a physical ticket printed at a terminal kiosk using your booking number (or birthdate). This is ultimately an extension of the "different apps" problem from ① — if you booked an intercity route, make it your default to arrive 20–30 minutes early and stop at the kiosk first.

⑦ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. I booked, but my route isn't in the app — wrong app. If it's not showing in the express bus apps (kobus, T-money), switch and search Bustago or TxBus instead. Check the table in ① again and it resolves in under a minute.

2. Can't find the boarding bay — if you can't see the departure board, show your ticket at the information desk or ticket window and just ask "what number?" — they'll point it out.

3. Missed the bus — for express buses on high-frequency routes (Seoul↔Busan, etc.), many ticket windows can switch you to a standby ticket for the next departure. Some intercity routes run infrequently, so the moment you miss one, head straight to the ticket window to check the next departure and refund.

⑧ Plan B — If You're Still Stuck

If the app or website keeps failing, go to the ticket window — this is the most reliable fallback. Even if you can't say the destination name out loud, show the place name from your map app or a note and the ticket window staff will find the right ticket for you. Even if your card is rejected, the window can usually take a different card or cash on the spot.
