① Why Everyone Waitlists with an App Instead of Standing in Line

Popular Korean restaurants and cafes still have a live "opening run" culture, where people line up before opening. But in just the last few years, the center of gravity has shifted fast — instead of standing in front of the restaurant, joining a remote waitlist on your phone and going off to a nearby cafe or sightseeing until a notification calls you back is becoming the standard. Miss this and you'll be the only one caught off guard by "there's no line outside, but it's still a one-hour wait to get in." In short, a waitlist here needs to be understood not as a visible line, but as a digital queue.

② The Tablet Waitlist Machine Outside — Master Flow

The first thing foreigners run into is the tablet waitlist machine placed at the entrance. Brands differ, but they rarely stray far from the flow below.

Step What's on screen What to do Trap
1 Standby screen Join Waitlist (웨이팅 등록) Tap the screen to start If the screen is off, tapping it wakes it up
2 "Select your party size (인원을 선택해주세요)" number stepper Set your party size with - +, then Next (다음) Some machines ask you to enter adults and children separately
3 "Please enter your phone number (전화번호를 입력해주세요)" number keypad Enter a Korean number (010-XXXX-XXXX format) The biggest barrier — many machines won't let you move to the next step without a Korean number
4 "You're registered (등록되었습니다)" + your position in line and estimated wait Take a photo of the screen or take a numbered ticket The estimated time is just a guideline — it commonly runs longer in practice
5 While waiting Wait near the restaurant, keeping an eye on the buzzer/vibration/screen updates Wander too far and you'll miss your call (see ⑤)
6 "Number OO, please come in (OO번 손님 입장해주세요)" call Go to the counter and tell them your number or show your screen Some restaurants give you only a short grace period after calling, so head over right away

3 Ways to Handle Not Having a Korean Number (a 3-step breakthrough)

1. Borrow a companion's Korean number — If anyone in your group is on a Korean SIM, register with their number. This is the fastest option.

2. Route around it with CatchTable Global — If the restaurant is a partner, join the waitlist remotely through the app and skip the tablet entirely (see ③).

3. Ask a staff member directly — Point at the tablet and convey "I don't have a phone number" through gestures plus a translation app. Staff often register you under the restaurant's own number, or just add you to a handwritten list instead.

③ CatchTable Global — The Complete Guide to Remote Waitlisting

CatchTable Global is the foreigner-only version built so you can use it without a Korean number.

  • Sign-up: Search "CatchTable Global" in the app store → sign up with a Google or Apple account (no Korean number needed)
  • Language: Supports English, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese
  • Payment: Overseas-issued cards can be registered
  • Coverage: Over 8,000 partner restaurants are searchable (not every restaurant is on it, so checking by search is essential)
  • Remote waitlist procedure: Search the restaurant → select party size → Join Waitlist (웨이팅 등록) → check the estimated wait → get an app notification as your turn approaches → arrive and go in
  • Remote waitlisting is deposit-free by default.

If the restaurant you're looking for isn't on CatchTable, don't hesitate — switch straight to the in-store tablet from ② or method 3 (asking staff directly).

⑤ If You Miss the Call — When Your Number Has Already Passed

  • App (CatchTable) waitlist: If you missed the notification, don't panic — take a screenshot of the app screen (your position status) and show it at the counter to ask them to check. Most places have some slack of a few parties, but it varies by restaurant policy, so check first.
  • In-store tablet: Bring your numbered ticket or a screenshot to the counter and show your number directly. Missing the announcement or buzzer doesn't automatically cancel your spot in most cases.
  • The common fix: Show "my number has already passed, please check" through a translation app while presenting your waitlist screen, and it's usually resolved on the spot.

⑥ No-Show Etiquette and the Deposit Concept

Korean restaurants tend to be sensitive about no-shows. Especially at popular restaurants and small establishments, more and more places require a deposit (예약금) at the formal reservation stage to prevent no-shows.

  • Remote waitlisting (a stand-in for lining up) is usually deposit-free by default, but a separate, formal table reservation can have different deposit policies depending on the restaurant.
  • If a reservation screen asks for payment information, that means a cancellation or no-show fee may apply — read the fine print.
  • If you can't make it, be sure to cancel in the app. A no-show can lead to penalties on future use.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. The tablet demands a phone number but you don't have a Korean one — Try the 3 methods from ② in order (a companion's number, CatchTable remote, asking staff directly).

2. You were spending time nearby and missed the call — Don't panic; show your waitlist screen or numbered ticket at the counter and ask them to check (see ⑤).

3. You joined the waitlist, but it's running way behind the estimate — Remember the estimate is just a guideline, and just periodically check that the app notification is still active while you go handle other plans.

⑨ Plan B — Timing Your Visit to Skip the Waitlist Entirely

  • Targeting right after opening is the surest bet (the "opening run"). Popular restaurants especially start forming lines 30 minutes to an hour before opening, but getting in right at opening is often possible without a waitlist app at all.
  • Target a quiet window just outside the lunch and dinner peaks.
  • Checking the estimated wait on CatchTable in advance, joining the waitlist, filling the time nearby, then returning when the notification comes is the least stressful approach.
