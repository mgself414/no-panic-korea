🎯 How to Read This Step Sheet — Notation Rules

It uses the exact notation set in #1270 the kiosk guide. This is the series-wide shared convention.

  • Buttons on a screen appear in brackets: Next (다음)
  • Screen titles and prompts appear in corner brackets: 「peureim-eul seontaekhaseyo (프레임을 선택하세요) — Please select a frame」
  • Actions you do by hand are bold: "Insert ₩1,000 bills one at a time"

The reason this piece exists is simple. There's a real case of a visitor with zero Korean missing a single checkbox on the payment screen inside an insaengnekeot (인생네컷) booth — the shoot went fine, but they walked out with only printed photos, no digital file. That moment of panic is the starting point for this series entry.

① A Few Seconds on What Insaengnekeot (인생네컷) Is — The Unmanned-Store Layout

Photo booths run unmanned 24 hours a day, like a convenience store. No staff doesn't mean it's hard.

  • Walk right in: open the door and go in. No reservation, no staff interaction needed.
  • Props are free: headbands, hats, glasses, and other items hanging on the wall or shelves are almost always free to use, no restrictions. When you're done shooting, just hang them back where they were.
  • Brands are marked by the storefront sign: the three most common chains nationwide are Life4Cuts (인생네컷), Photoism (포토이즘), and Harufilm (하루필름). The screen UI differs slightly by brand (see ⑦), but the procedural skeleton is the same.

② Finding a Booth — Numbers and "In Use" Signs

Stores often have several booths side by side (Photo 01, Photo 02…).

  • If a light or screen above the booth entrance reads sayongjung (사용중) — "In Use" — move to another booth.
  • If the light is off or there's no light at all, the booth is free — pull back the curtain and go in.
  • If there's a line, wait your turn by number. Koreans wait their turn too, so there's no need to feel self-conscious.

③ The Master Flow — One Checkbox on the Payment Screen Is Everything

The sequence below doesn't change across brands. Step 5 is the heart of this piece.

Step What You See on Screen What to Do Trap
1 Standby screen (a "touch here" animation) Touch anywhere on the screen Even if the screen looks off, touching it wakes it up
2 Language selection English or another flag/language button Many stores have an English button, but older machines may be Korean-only
3 Frame selection (basic, seasonal, collab) Touch the frame design you want Frames differ in how many photo slots they have — check it matches your group size
4 Number of people & print count Select number of people, and how many prints More prints means a higher payment amount too
5 Payment screen — QR / digital file option Make sure the checkbox is checked ⚠️ The biggest trap: miss this checkbox and you'll only get printed photos — the digital file (QR) is never issued at all, with no way to undo it afterward
6 Payment method selection Card Pay (결제) or cash slot Cash only recognizes ₩1,000 bills. Card usually works fine — don't feed it large bills
7 Shooting-start instructions Position yourself as the screen directs Once the countdown starts, there's no cancel or pause button

Step 5 bears repeating. On the payment screen there's a checkbox next to text like 「QR daunrodeu poham (QR 다운로드 포함) — QR download included」 or something similar. If it isn't already checked, you must check it. There's no way to undo it once shooting starts.

④ The Shooting Stage — The Countdown Doesn't Stop

Once payment finishes, the screen switches to shooting mode.

  • It takes 8–10 shots in total, and you pick your favorites afterward (see ⑤).
  • Each shot gets about a 10-second countdown on screen, and the shutter fires the moment it hits zero.
  • Panic point: once it starts, there's no stopping or reshooting mid-way. If you're planning to use props, have them in hand before the countdown starts, and plan your poses for each shot ahead of time.
  • The on-screen prompt (something like 「pojeu-reul chwihaseyo (포즈를 취하세요) — Strike a pose」) is your cue for the next shot — when the text changes, a new countdown has started.

⑤ After Shooting — Choosing and Arranging Shots, Then Waiting to Print

Once shooting ends, the screen switches to edit mode.

1. Of the 8–10 shots taken, touch to select as many as your frame has slots for (usually 4).

2. Arrange your selected shots into the slots you want in the frame (some machines let you reorder them after touching).

3. If there are filter/retouch options, adjust them to taste, then press Done (완료) or Print (인쇄).

4. While printing is in progress, a QR code appears on screen or beside the output slot — this is the digital file you checked for in step 5 of ③.

The QR expires after 24 hours. It's safest to scan the QR right there on the spot and finish the download before you leave. Trying to do it later at your accommodation and forgetting is, in practice, the single most common mistake.

The minimum vocabulary that also works on older, Korean-only screens.

seontaek (선택) Select — choosing your shots
baechi (배치) Layout — arranging shots in the frame
gyeolje (결제) Payment

daunrodeu (다운로드) Download — after you scan the QR

cheoeumeuro (처음으로) is only a safe reset up until payment (step 6 of ③). After payment, shooting proceeds automatically, so there's no way to undo it.

⑦ Screen Differences Across the 3 Brands — A Mini Comparison

All three share the same procedural skeleton (③), but the screen tone and emphasis differ. Specific UI details can vary somewhat by store and machine model.

Brand Positioning What Stands Out On Screen
Life4Cuts (인생네컷) Most locations nationwide, the most mainstream The most frame variety and the most basic UI. Most beginner-friendly if it's your first time
Photoism (포토이즘) Pastel tones, strong idol/character collaborations Season and collab frame banners often appear large at the top of the screen
Harufilm (하루필름) Film-look aesthetics, tone correction emphasis The post-shoot retouch (filter) step is relatively more prominent

At all three, step 5 of ③ (the QR checkbox) and the payment methods of ⑥ (₩1,000 bills / card) apply the same way.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens, and How to Handle It

1. The countdown feels like it keeps going faster — that's normal. 10 seconds is shorter than it feels. Rather than rethinking your pose every shot, it's easier to pick 2–3 poses upfront and cycle through them.

2. The QR isn't scanning — first check whether a QR reappears near the print slot or on screen. If the screen has already moved on, you likely missed the checkbox in step 5 of ③ — in that case, the digital file can't be recovered. For your next shoot, check the checkbox first before you start.

3. A ₩5,000 bill won't fit in the cash slot — that's normal. Most machines only recognize ₩1,000 bills. Break it into ₩1,000 bills at a convenience store or nearby shop and try again, or switch to card.
