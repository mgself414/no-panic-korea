🎯 How to Read This Procedure Sheet — Screen Notation Rules

This piece isn't about "what Korean healthcare is like" — it writes down exactly "what to do, in what order, standing at the reception window when you're sick." The notation rules reuse the same 3 types finalized in #1270 as-is.

  • Buttons on screens and machines go in square brackets: Register
  • Window signs and document titles go in corner brackets: jeopsu-sunap (접수·수납)
  • Physical actions are in bold: "hand your passport to the window"

⚠️ Check this first: if someone is unconscious, struggling to breathe, bleeding that won't stop, or having severe chest pain, do not follow this sheet — call 119 immediately. Emergency response is handled entirely by #405 Emergency Hospital & Safety Guide. This piece is for non-emergencies only.

② Where to Go — uiwon (의원) vs byeongwon (병원), and Travelers Almost Always Want the First

Korean medical facilities are named by size. For a cold or an upset stomach on a trip, the answer is nearly always uiwon (의원).

Type Sign says Appointment Fit for travelers
Clinic — uiwon (의원) naegwa (내과), ibiinhugwa (이비인후과) and similar Walk in, no appointment ★★★ colds, stomach trouble, minor injuries
Hospital — byeongwon (병원) byeongwon (병원) Longer waits depending on department ★ when a clinic refers you upward
Tertiary hospital university hospital Effectively impossible without a referral ✕ going straight there is a wasted trip
  • Cold, sore throat → naegwa (내과) or ibiinhugwa (이비인후과)
  • Stomach ache → naegwa (내과)
  • Skin trouble → pibugwa (피부과)
  • Sprains and bruises → jeonghyeongoegwa (정형외과)

Search your map app for naegwa (내과) and clinics near your accommodation will come up. If the map app itself is the obstacle, read #1295 first.

Starting at a big hospital costs you. University hospitals will refuse registration without a referral, or charge far more. For mild symptoms a neighborhood clinic is faster and cheaper.

③ The 6 Steps from Reception to Pharmacy — That's the Whole Thing

Step Where What to do The trap
1 jeopsu-sunap (접수·수납) window Hand over your passport and say it's your first visit If there's a number-ticket machine, take a ticket first
2 The window Fill in a short personal-details form Often Korean-only → see ⑤ for translation apps
3 Waiting room Wait until your name is called Your name is called with Korean pronunciation — tell the window in advance if you may not catch it
4 Consultation room Explain symptoms to the doctor, usually 10–20 minutes Showing a phrase card from ⑤ is faster
5 jeopsu-sunap (접수·수납) window Pay on the spot and receive your cheobangjeon (처방전) Settle payment before you leave
6 A nearby pharmacy Hand over the prescription and receive your medicine The clinic does not give you medicine

Step 6 is the single most important thing in this piece. In Korea doctors diagnose and pharmacists dispense, so you almost never walk out of a clinic holding medicine. You receive a piece of paper — the prescription — and take it outside to a pharmacy. Travelers who don't know this assume they were given nothing and go back to the hotel empty-handed.

The pharmacy is usually on the ground floor of the same building or straight across the street. Look for a green cross or a yak (약) sign. Everything that happens inside the pharmacy is covered by #1278.

④ The Prescription — a Slip of Paper with an Expiry Date

  • The prescription works at any pharmacy. You are not tied to one attached to the clinic.
  • It has a validity window. Usually 7 days from the date of issue, but clinics may write a shorter one such as 3 days — check the sayonggigan (사용기간) printed on the paper yourself.
  • Once it expires you cannot collect the medicine and would need another consultation. Going straight to a pharmacy the same day is safest.
  • You normally receive two copies — one to hand to the pharmacy, one to keep. Take both.

Don't throw away your copy of the prescription and the receipts — you'll need them for travel-insurance claims after you get home.

⑤ When the Language Doesn't Work — Try in This Order

1. Translate your symptom sentence in advance and show it on your screen at the reception window and in the consultation room. These are worth saving before you go:

  • gamgi jeungsang-i isseoyo (감기 증상이 있어요) — "I have cold symptoms"
  • baega apayo (배가 아파요) — "My stomach hurts"
  • yeoli nayo (열이 나요) — "I have a fever"
  • mogi apayo (목이 아파요) — "My throat hurts"
  • yeohaengja-imnida. geongangboheom-i eopseoyo (여행자입니다. 건강보험이 없어요) — "I'm a traveler. I don't have health insurance"

2. 1339 — a multilingual health and emergency hotline. It can also point you to a nearby clinic or pharmacy.

3. e-gen.or.kr — the official portal for finding clinics and pharmacies that are open right now. Especially useful at night and on public holidays.

4. Medical interpretation — the Medical Korea Support Center connects professional medical interpreters in languages including English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, and Arabic.

5. Large hospitals in Seoul and Busan have international clinics with English-speaking coordinators. They cost more and take longer than a neighborhood clinic, though — for mild symptoms you don't need one.

⑥ Cost — What It Comes To Without Korean Insurance

Korean residents pay only a fraction through national health insurance. Travelers have no such coverage and pay the full amount — roughly 3 to 5 times what an insured resident pays.

Item Uninsured traveler, approximate
Neighborhood clinic consultation ₩30,000 – ₩80,000
Prescription medicine at the pharmacy ₩5,000 – ₩15,000
  • It varies a lot by clinic, department, and whether tests are run. These are ranges, not fixed prices.
  • Tests such as X-rays or blood work push it higher. Asking what a test will cost before agreeing to it is not rude.
  • Cards work at most clinics. A few small ones are cash-only, so keep some cash on hand — see #1301.
  • If you have travel insurance, keep the receipt, the itemized statement, and the prescription. You'll need all three to claim.

jeonghyeongoegwa (정형외과) Orthopedics — sprains, bruises

jillyobi yeongsujeung (진료비 영수증) Receipt — for insurance claims, don't discard

Tip: you don't need all 13. Knowing four gets the whole procedure into view — jeopsu (접수) (the start) · cheobangjeon (처방전) (what you leave with) · yakguk (약국) (where you go next) · naegwa (내과) (the department for a cold or stomach trouble).

⑧ Three Panic Scenarios

1. The consultation ended and nobody gave you medicine. That's normal. Clinics and pharmacies are separated in Korea, so the clinic hands you only the cheobangjeon (처방전). Take that paper downstairs or across the street to a pharmacy. Travelers who read it as "they gave me nothing" end up back at the hotel still sick.

2. The university hospital wouldn't register you. Tertiary hospitals require a referral from another facility. It isn't a malfunction and it isn't discrimination. For a cold or stomach trouble a neighborhood uiwon (의원) was the right destination anyway, and it's faster and cheaper. Search naegwa (내과) on your map app instead.

3. You've been handed a form and can't read a character of it. Reception forms usually ask only for name, date of birth, contact, and symptoms. Point your translation app's camera at it and it resolves immediately. Showing your passport and asking staff for help is fine too — traveler registrations are not unusual.
