🎯 How to Read This Procedure Sheet — Screen Notation Rules

This piece isn't about "why there are so many unmanned stores" — it writes down exactly "what to do, in what order, standing in front of the self-checkout screen." The notation rules reuse the same 3 types finalized in #1270 as-is.

  • Buttons on screen appear in brackets: Payment (결제)
  • Screen titles and notices appear in corner brackets: 「yeollyeong hwagin (연령 확인) — Age check」
  • Actions you do by hand are bold: "tag your card on the reader"

① This Piece Is "Machine Operation Procedure" Only — a Different Angle from #1219

#1219 is a culture overview of unmanned, automated retail spanning unmanned cafes, unmanned photo studios, and quirky vending machines. If you're wondering "why does Korea have so many unmanned stores," read #1219 first — that's the right order.

This piece assumes you already know that culture, and covers only what actually happens when you stand in front of the self-checkout screen at a convenience store, Daiso, Olive Young, and the like — night-entry authentication, the 4 checkout steps, what to do when a foreign card won't work, and what to do when the screen stops at alcohol or tobacco. Unmanned cafes and quirky vending machines aren't covered here.

② At Night, Even the Door Is Different — Hybrid Unmanned Switchover and Entrance Card Tag

Self-checkout has spread widely, from convenience stores and supermarkets to larger stores like Daiso and Olive Young. Many stores run as a hybrid — staffed during the day, switching to unmanned at night.

Once a store switches to unmanned mode, the entrance door itself is locked. To get in:

  • Tag your card on the door reader — the door only opens once this reads.
  • Foreign-issued cards are recognized in many cases. But it's not a 100% guarantee.
  • A door that won't open isn't a malfunction — not knowing this authentication step exists is the real problem. Knowing just this one sentence is enough to keep you from being caught off guard in front of a convenience store at night.

③ The 4 Self-Checkout Steps — This Order at Any Store

Step What to Do Trap
1 Enter (at a night unmanned store, start with the card tag from ②) Daytime, staffed stores skip this step
2 Scan each product barcode on the scanner one at a time Multiple items? Scan them one by one, in order
3 If you need a Bag (봉투), select it on screen before payment Bags aren't added automatically — it's common to realize only after paying that you have no bag
4 Payment (결제) → payment complete → leave For payment methods, see ④

Step 3 is the key one. A bag doesn't come automatically just because you scanned your items — if you need one, you have to select it yourself before pressing the payment button.

④ The 3-Step Response for Foreign Cards — Don't Keep Swiping When It Fails

Order Try If It Fails
① Tag (contactless) Move to the next step
② Insert Move to the next step
③ If it still fails, move to a staffed counter Repeated attempts are a bad idea

The same card can genuinely work at Store A and fail at Store B — that doesn't mean the card is broken, it's a difference between store terminals. Try ①tag → ②insert, up to two attempts only, and on the third don't keep retrying — go straight to the staffed counter. If it's a night unmanned store with no counter, it's faster to just move to a nearby store or convenience store.

⑤ If It Stops at Alcohol or Tobacco — That's the Normal Procedure

  • Alcohol and tobacco either can't be self-checked out at all, or require staff approval. If the screen stops, it's not a malfunction — it's this verification step.
  • If 「yeollyeong hwagin (연령 확인) — Age check」 appears, have a photo ID such as your passport ready — checking age is a normal procedure, not you being suspected of anything.
  • For pharmacy questions or payment issues that just won't resolve, don't force it through the machine — the staffed counter is the answer.

⑥ Self-Checkout Screen Survival Words

Romanization (Korean) Meaning
kadeu taegeu (카드 태그) Card tag — night entrance-door authentication
bakodeu seukaen (바코드 스캔) Barcode scan — the product-scanning step
bongtu (봉투) Bag — must be selected yourself before payment to get one

gyeolje (결제) Payment — the button that proceeds with checkout
kadeu sabip (카드 삽입) Card insert — retry option if tag fails

yeollyeong hwagin (연령 확인) Age check — the screen stops here for alcohol/tobacco

Tip: you don't need to memorize all 12. Knowing just four gets the whole procedure into view — kadeu taegeu (카드 태그) (entry) · bongtu (봉투) (the easy-to-miss trap) · yeollyeong hwagin (연령 확인) (why it stopped) · kadeu oryu (카드 오류) (the signal to move to the counter).

⑦ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. The convenience store door won't open at night — you assumed it was a store you could just walk into without authentication, but it's locked. Not knowing you have to tag your card on the door reader to open it, it's easy to just turn around and leave. If the reader isn't obvious, take one more look around the door.

2. You scanned a beer and the screen froze — it's not a malfunction, it's the 「yeollyeong hwagin (연령 확인) — Age check」 procedure. Show your passport or press the call-staff button and it clears. At a night unmanned store there may be no staffed counter for this step, so it's safer to have your ID ready from the start.

3. A foreign card won't work and you're swiping it a fifth time — this is where you need to stop. Repeated attempts can lead to a temporary card lock. Try ①tag ②insert, two attempts only, and from the third attempt switch straight to the counter or a different card.
