🎯 How to Read This Manual — Screen Notation Rules

This piece doesn't tell you "what to do" — it writes down, exactly, "what appears on the screen, which button to press, and in what order." Read it once in bed before you leave home, and the screen in front of the machine will no longer be one you're seeing for the first time.

  • Buttons on screen appear in brackets: Takeout (포장)
  • Screen titles and prompts appear in quotes: "Select order type (주문 방법 선택)"
  • Actions you do by hand are bold: "Insert the card into the slot below, all the way"

① Why Korean Restaurants Have Machines Instead of Staff

Korea's food service industry automated ordering very quickly, as rising labor costs collided with a preference for contactless service. Major fast-food chains like McDonald's, Lotteria, and Burger King now use kiosks as the default order counter in most locations, and budget coffee chains (Mega Coffee, Compose Coffee, etc.) commonly run stores that simply don't take orders without a kiosk. Korean BBQ restaurants and casual snack-food joints are shifting toward tablets built into the table.

In other words, "there's a machine" doesn't mean "unfriendly service" — the machine simply is the counter. Understand that structure going in, and you're already halfway there. This manual is the other half.

② The 3 Kiosk Types — Which One Is In Front of You

Type Where you'll find it Language Difficulty
A. Global fast-food type McDonald's, Burger King, KFC 🇺🇸🇨🇳🇯🇵 switchable via flag icon ★☆☆
B. Korean cafe/burger type Mega Coffee, Compose Coffee, Lotteria, Mom's Touch Often Korean-only (even where an English button exists, only part of it is translated) ★★★
C. Table-order tablet type Korean BBQ joints, Kimbap Heaven, pubs — tablets built into the table Varies by store, big photos ★★☆
Note Convenience store self-checkout is a separate topic → see #1219 unmanned-store edition
  • Type A: Look first for the flag icon in a corner of the screen, or a English button. If it's there, you don't need half of this article.
  • Type B: The main target of this manual. Break through with the 15 survival words in ④ below.
  • Type C: The least stressful, since you can sit and tap through it slowly. Just remember that nothing reaches the kitchen until you press Order (주문하기) at the end.

③ The Universal 6-Step Master Flow — Same Order, Every Machine

No matter the brand, Korean kiosks never stray from the 6 steps below.

Step What's on screen Button to press Trap
1 "Eating in? (매장에서 드시나요?)" Eat In (매장) or Takeout (포장) Some machines skip this screen (and ask at the end instead)
2 Grid of menu photos + category tabs on the left Tap the photo of what you want The photo IS the language — you can order without reading a word
3 "Choose options (옵션 선택)" popup (combo/size, shots/syrup) Item Only (단품) vs Combo (세트), then Confirm (선택 완료) after options You can't move on until the options popup is closed — if you're stuck, this is the screen 9 times out of 10
4 "Cart (장바구니)" (items added + total) Add more, or Pay (결제하기) Pay (결제하기) is the big button at the very bottom of the screen
5 "Points/Coupon (적립/쿠폰)" popup Skip (건너뛰기) / No (아니오) Tourists should skip this 100% of the time — it's a Korean phone-number entry screen
6 "Choose payment method (결제 수단 선택)" → insert card Card Payment (카드 결제) → insert the card chip-up, all the way in Wait a few seconds without moving it. If you hear a beep telling you to remove it, pull it out

When it's done, a number prints on your receipt. When that number appears on the wall monitor, pick up your order at the counter. That's the whole process.

④ 15 Survival Words for Korean-Only Screens — "See This, It Means This"

These are the exact words you'll actually run into on Type B (Korean-only) machines. Screenshot them, or save this page.

On-screen Korean Meaning = what to do
maejang (매장) Eat in — when you're dining there
pojang (포장) Takeout — when you're taking it to go
damgi (담기) Add to cart
jangbaguni (장바구니) Cart — the list of what you've added
danpum (단품) Item only (not a combo)
seteu (세트) Combo/set
opsyeon (옵션) Options popup
seontaek wallyo (선택 완료) Done/Confirm — closes the popup
gyeoljehagi (결제하기) Pay — the single most important button
jeongnip (적립) Points — irrelevant to tourists
geonneottwigi (건너뛰기) Skip — your exit from the points screen
pumjeol (품절) Sold out — grayed-out menu item
yeongsujeung (영수증) Receipt — doubles as your pickup number
cheoeumeuro (처음으로) Start over — resets a wrong tap
hochul (호출) Call staff

Tip: you don't need to memorize all 15. The three core verbs are gyeoljehagi (결제하기) — go, geonneottwigi (건너뛰기) — escape, and cheoeumeuro (처음으로) — reset.

⑤ The Payment Fork — When Your Overseas Card Gets Rejected, That's the Real Story

Korea's payment network (VAN) runs on its own proprietary standard, so only a minority of terminals support the international EMV standard. That's why the same Visa or Mastercard can be rejected at the kiosk but approved at the counter — this happens all the time. It isn't a problem with your card; it's a quirk of Korea's payment infrastructure.

Situation What to do
The kiosk spits your card back out Retry once with insert instead of tap (contactless) → if it still fails, head straight for the counter without a second thought
An unmanned store with no counter Try a different card (different issuer) → check whether the machine also takes cash (look for a bill slot)
If you're a repeat traveler Get a WOWPASS (a prepaid card for foreigners, issued from machines at major stations and the airport) or preload a T-money balance → see the payment deep-dive #527 for details
Cash only, but the machine is card-only Call staff (the Call Staff (호출) button, or the counter) — most will process the payment manually for you
  • Samsung Pay / Apple Pay: more stores accept Apple Pay these days, but it's still hit-or-miss. Two physical cards (from different issuers) is the safest combination.
  • Carry a backup of 30,000–50,000 won in cash at all times, and no rejection will rattle you.

⑥ Real Walkthroughs by Chain — The Exact Screens at Each Store

🍔 McDonald's (Type A, English available)

1. Tap the flag icon at the bottom of the first screen → English

2. "Eat In or Take Out?" → choose one

3. Menu → tap a burger → "Meal or Item Only?" → options (you can swap the fries/drink)

4. Checkout → points screen Skip → insert card

  • Note: the translation here is the most complete of any chain. McDonald's is the ideal place to rehearse your first kiosk experience.

🍔 Lotteria (closer to Type B — has an English button, but only partly translated)

1. Switch language if there's a button for it — though some menu names stay in Korean either way

2. "Eat In/Takeout (매장/포장)" → tap the menu photo → choosing Item Only (단품) / Combo (세트) is a mandatory popup

3. Pay (결제하기) → points popup Skip (건너뛰기) → insert card

  • Note: the combo-options popup has more steps than McDonald's. If you're stuck, look somewhere on screen for Confirm (선택 완료).

☕ Mega Coffee (Type B, mostly Korean-only)

1. Many stores have no language button at all — break through head-on with the ④ survival words

2. "Eat In/Takeout (매장/포장)" → tap the coffee photo → options popup: HOT / ICE, size, Add Shot (샷 추가)

3. Add to Cart (담기) → Pay (결제하기) → points phone-number screen Skip (건너뛰기) → card

  • Note: budget coffee chains (Compose Coffee, Paik's Coffee, etc.) share almost the exact same flow. Learn one and you can handle them all.

⑦ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens, and How to Stay Calm

1. The line behind you is long — Even Koreans take 1–2 minutes on average. Rushing people from behind isn't part of the culture, so take a breath. If it feels like too much pressure, step aside, re-read the ③ flow on this page, and try again — no one will say a word.

2. You tapped the wrong thing and now the screen looks strange — The universal fix: Start Over (처음으로) or the ← in the upper left. Nothing is finalized on a kiosk until you actually pay.

3. You paused, and it went back to the first screen — Many machines auto-reset after 30 seconds to a minute of no input. It's not broken. Just start again from the beginning.

⑧ Plan B — If the Machine Just Won't Cooperate

  • Check if counter ordering is possible: If a staff member is stationed at the counter and there's a POS terminal in sight, most places will take a verbal order. Some locations even post a "Counter ordering available (카운터 주문 가능)" sign next to the kiosk.
  • One sentence to call staff: "Dowajuseyo (도와주세요)" — "help me" — say this one phrase and point at the kiosk, and you're set. Korean staff are used to kiosk help requests.
  • Showing the screen instead of speaking: Translate "I'd like to order this. Card payment doesn't work." into Korean with Papago or Google Translate and just show the screen → for app setup, see the essential-apps edition #1230.
  • Simulate it with a companion: Easiest for your first real attempt is having one person in your group read the ③ table on this page aloud while the other one presses the buttons.
