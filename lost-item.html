🎯 How to Read This Manual — Notation Conventions

The most common mistake when you lose something is reporting it anywhere and just waiting. In Korea, the counter you need depends on where you lost it, and reporting to the wrong place just burns time. This guide covers only one thing: for each place, where to go and what to bring.

  • A button on a site or screen goes in brackets: Search Found Items
  • Signage and noticeboard text goes in guillemets: 「Lost and Found Center (yusilmul senteo / 유실물센터)」
  • A hands-on action is bold: report to the Station Office (yeongmusil / 역무실) first
  • A sentence to show staff exactly as-is goes in code: 가방을 두고 내렸어요 (gabang-eul dugo naeryeosseoyo)

⚠ Notation Principle: The hours and storage periods below are the official guidance as of September 2026. Practices that vary by person, like reward amounts, are not listed. Passport and card reissue requirements differ by country, so this guide covers only the order of steps and leaves the details to your embassy or card issuer.

① First Steps — Decide This Within 3 Minutes

The moment you realize something's missing, retrace your memory and settle just two things. Once these are set, you'll know where to go.

What to Decide Why
Where you lost it Subway, taxi, bus, airport, and shops each have a different counter
When (roughly what time) For taxis and buses, you need to pin down the time slot and vehicle to search

A payment record makes this much stronger. Your card statement and taxi app ride history keep the boarding time and vehicle on record. Take a screenshot of it.

② If You Lost It on the Subway — Station Office → Lost and Found Center, in Order

Seoul's subway has a clear sequence.

1. Report it to the Station Office at the nearest station. If the item turns up, this report is what connects it to you.

2. Check with the Lost and Found Center for that line. Found items stay at the center for 7 days.

3. After 7 days, it's handed over to the jurisdictional police station and kept for about 9 months. From this point, it's the police who track it down.

Lost and Found Center Lines Covered
Sicheong Station (시청역) Lines 1·2
Chungmuro Station (충무로역) Lines 3·4
Wangsimni Station (왕십리역) Lines 5·8
Taereung Iphgu Station (태릉입구역) Lines 6·7

Hours are weekdays 09:00–18:00; closed Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays. If you lost something on a weekend night, that means waiting until Monday morning. Still, file the report at the Station Office in the meantime.

Tip for foreign visitors — If you're not sure which line, don't search by station name; remember the color of the line you rode. If you transferred, report it to every segment you rode.

③ If You Lost It in a Taxi or Bus — It All Comes Down to Identifying the Vehicle

For taxis and buses, it all comes down to whether you can pin down one specific vehicle.

Taxi

  • If you called it with Kakao T: open that ride in the app's ride history and try contacting the driver through it. If that doesn't connect, escalate to Kakao T customer service.
  • If you hailed it on the street: the vehicle plate number on your receipt or card statement is the key. In Seoul, the 120 Dasan Call Center (120 다산콜센터) can look up the company from the plate number.
  • Private and corporate taxis have different contacts — private taxis go through the local association, corporate taxis go through the company itself. 120 will tell you which one applies.

Bus

  • In Seoul, the 120 Dasan Call Center confirms which company operates the route.
  • When you contact the company, you need to give the bus number, boarding and alighting times, the stop where you got off, and a description of the item for them to search. Write down these four things first.

Tip for foreign visitors — 120 is a Korean-language-first line. If language is a concern, it's faster to call the 1330 Tourist Hotline first and get connected through an interpreter (#1299).

④ If You Lost It at the Airport or a Shop

Incheon International Airport

Terminal Location Hours
Terminal 1 Lost and Found Office (yusilmul gwalliso / 유실물관리소), B1 07:00–21:30, every day of the year
Terminal 2 Inside the Central Government Complex, 2F general area 07:00–21:30, every day of the year

Something lost on the plane itself goes through the airline, not the airport. Airport lost items can also be searched on Gyeongchal Minwon 24 (경찰민원24).

Shops and Cafes

  • Going back to the shop or calling is fastest. The shop holds found items briefly, then hands them to the police.
  • By law, whoever finds an item must report and hand it to police within 7 days. So after a few days have passed, it's more likely with the police than the shop.

⑤ Searching Nationwide — Gyeongchal Minwon 24

This is the integrated search you use when you can't visit every counter individually. The former Lost112 service merged into Gyeongchal Minwon 24 (minwon24.police.go.kr) as of January 2026.

  • You can search found items with no sign-up required.
  • It offers a menu in over 40 languages.
  • To file a loss report yourself online, you do need to sign up.
  • If Korean is a barrier, there's guidance connecting you to a government phone interpretation service and 1330.

Tip for foreign visitors — Search by item type, the date you lost it, and roughly where. Registration takes time, so check again a day later.

⑥ If You Lost Your Passport — There's a Fixed Order

This isn't just a lost item — it's a matter of identity, so you have to follow the order.

1. File a loss report at a Korean police station and get the receipt. This receipt (jeopsujeung / 접수증) is your ticket to the next step.

2. Contact your home country's embassy or consulate. Reissue or emergency passport requirements differ by country, so follow your own mission's guidance.

3. If your departure is coming up soon, mention that first.

This guide does not list the required documents — they vary by country, and getting it wrong means a wasted trip.

⑦ If You Lost Your Card — Freezing It Comes First

1. Contact your card issuer and freeze it immediately. For a card issued abroad, call the global lost-card number on the back of the card or in the issuer's app. Even without a Korean number, you can call through the app or an internet phone service.

2. File a loss/theft report at a Korean police station. You may need the receipt for compensation or an insurance claim.

3. Compensation terms for fraudulent use follow your card issuer's policy — this can differ from Korean card standards, so confirm directly with your issuer.

Tip for foreign visitors — If your cash gets tied up, you're stuck. If you only carry one card, the moment you freeze it, check your route to cash first (#1286·#1301).

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. Heading straight to the Lost and Found Center on a weekend — Seoul's 4 subway lost-and-found centers are open weekday daytime only. On weekends, all you can do is file the report at the Station Office.

2. Asking about a taxi with no plate number — without a plate number or app ride history, a search is nearly impossible. Find your payment record first.

3. Taking your passport to the embassy first — the police receipt comes first. Get the order wrong and you'll have to come back.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. How long do they hold onto it? — At the subway, the center holds it for 7 days, then it's transferred to a police station and kept for about 9 months. The counter changes as time passes.

2. Do I need to pay a reward? — It's a common practice, but this guide doesn't list an amount. It's not a legally fixed sum — it varies from person to person.

3. Can I file a report without speaking Korean? — Gyeongchal Minwon 24's found-item search supports over 40 languages, and the phone line connects you through 1330 interpretation.
