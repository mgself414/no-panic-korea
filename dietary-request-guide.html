🎯 How to Read This Manual — Notation Rules

The core of this piece isn't "speaking" — it's "showing." It's built so that even if your pronunciation doesn't land, holding up a screen or a printed card is enough on its own.

  • Sentences to show as-is appear in bold quotes: "Deol maepge hae juseyo"
  • Romanization is split into its own table column instead of italics in parentheses
  • Screen/app prompts appear in corner brackets: 「yocheongsahang (요청사항) — Requests」

① Why You Need This Card

Korean food's default seasoning is gochujang and gochugaru (red pepper paste/flakes), so "not spicy" is the exception and "spicy" is the default. On top of that, what a Korean person means by "it's not spicy" often runs hotter than a foreign visitor's baseline — so if you only confirm it verbally, you can still end up caught off guard. Then there's the dietary categories already well established in the West, like vegetarian and halal, which are still relatively unfamiliar territory for Korea's food-service structure — ingredients like fermented seafood (jeotgal) or meat-based broth are baked into the cooking process as "obviously included," so they never surface unless you ask explicitly. That's why this piece skips pronunciation drills and goes straight for a card you pull up on screen or print out and hand over.

② 12 Show-Me Card Phrases — Just Show the Screen and You're Done

Screenshot this table or save a capture of it to your gallery. Showing it to staff on-screen before you order is the most reliable move.

Romanization (한글) Meaning
Deol maepge hae juseyo (덜 맵게 해 주세요) Less spicy, please
An maepge hae juseyo (안 맵게 해 주세요) Not spicy at all, please
Maewoyo? (매워요?) Is this spicy?
Igeo ppae juseyo (이거 빼 주세요) Please leave this out
Jeoneun chaesikjuuijayeyo (저는 채식주의자예요) I'm a vegetarian
Jeoneun bigeonieyo (저는 비건이에요) I'm vegan
Hallalingayo? (할랄인가요?) Is this halal?
Dwaejigogiga deureoganayo? (돼지고기가 들어가나요?) Does it contain pork?
Gyeongwaryu allereugiga isseoyo (견과류 알레르기가 있어요) I have a nut allergy
Gapgangnyu allereugiga isseoyo (갑각류 알레르기가 있어요) I have a shellfish allergy
Geulluteni eomnayo? (글루텐이 없나요?) Is it gluten-free?
Jeotgari deureoganayo? (젓갈이 들어가나요?) Does it contain fermented seafood?

> Tip: you don't need to memorize all 12. Just save 2–3 that match your situation and pin them to the front of your photo gallery — that's enough.

③ The Hidden-Ingredient Trap Table — the Truth Behind "Vegan, But Kimchi"

Korean food has an unusually high rate of visible ingredients not matching what's actually in the dish. These 4 are the ones travelers get caught by most often.

Dish Hidden ingredient Why it's a trap
Kimchi Jeotgal (젓갈) — salted shrimp, anchovy fish sauce, and similar fermented seafood Looks like "pickled vegetables," but the fermenting seasoning almost always includes seafood-based sauce — catches out both vegans and seafood-allergy travelers
Soup dishes (doenjang-jjigae/된장찌개 soybean paste stew, sundubu-jjigae/순두부찌개 soft tofu stew, yukgaejang/육개장 spicy beef soup, etc.) Anchovy/kelp broth, or beef-bone (sagol) broth Even if only vegetables are floating on top, the base broth is usually animal-derived
Namul (나물) seasoned vegetable side dishes Occasionally seasoned with jeotgal or fish sauce Contrary to the assumption that "namul = vegetables," a small amount of fermented seafood sometimes goes into the seasoning
Tteokbokki (떡볶이) and stir-fried sauces Fish sauce or eomuk (fish cake) Hidden behind the spicy seasoning, undetectable by taste alone
  • How to check: use the "Jeotgari deureoganayo? (젓갈이 들어가나요?)" line from the table in ② for kimchi, namul, or any soup dish.

④ Vegan/Halal Restaurant Search Procedure

1. Naver Map: type "vegan (비건)" or "halal (할랄)" into the category search box — it has the highest real-world usage density of any Korean map app, and review photos let you check the menu before you go.

2. Dedicated apps: vegan-specific apps like HappyCow keep steadily building out Seoul restaurant data — cross-checking against Naver Map improves accuracy.

3. Moving to a dense neighborhood widens your options even without searching.

Diet Dense neighborhoods
Vegan Hongdae, Itaewon, Gangnam
Halal Myeongdong, Itaewon
  • Itaewon is the only neighborhood where both categories overlap — if you're short on time, starting there is the most efficient move.

⑤ How to Use an Allergy Card — Showing It Before You Order Works Best

  • Timing is everything: show the card the moment you sit down, before you've settled on a menu. It's far more reliable than complaining after the food arrives, and it also gives staff room to adjust before cooking even starts.
  • Icons + Korean text works best — a card with icons (a nut, a shrimp, a wheat stalk, etc.) alongside the text gets understood at a glance even by busy staff, more than a text-only card.
  • If you have a severe allergy (anaphylaxis, etc.), even with a card in hand, it's worth consulting a doctor beforehand to prepare a response plan.

⑥ Using the Delivery App's Request Field — Ordering Without Saying a Word

The same card phrases work just as well when you order through a delivery app instead of visiting in person. Both Baemin (배달의민족) and Coupang Eats have a 「yocheongsahang (요청사항) — Requests」 field before checkout — for the detailed steps to get there, see #1272 Delivery app real-use guide.

  • Copy-paste the phrases from the table in ② straight into the request field: Deol maepge hae juseyo (덜 맵게 해 주세요), Igeo ppae juseyo (이거 빼 주세요), Gyeongwaryu allereugiga isseoyo (견과류 알레르기가 있어요)
  • Since delivery removes real-time back-and-forth with staff, putting it in writing is actually more reliable than in-person ordering.

⑦ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens and How to Handle It

1. You realize it's spicy only after ordering — once cooking has started, canceling is hard. The realistic move is to set aside the broth or sauce the moment it arrives and neutralize the heat with mild side dishes like plain rice or steamed egg.

2. You showed the card and they still don't seem to get it — staff might be busy, or the icon might have been unclear. Don't panic — hold the card up again, squarely, and point to the relevant word with your finger. If that still doesn't work, showing it to a different staff member isn't rude.

3. They said "not spicy" and it's spicy — Korean spice tolerance often runs hotter than a foreign visitor's baseline. At a restaurant you're trying for the first time, don't fully trust an "it's not spicy" answer — tasting a small amount first is a habit that cuts down on bad surprises.

⑧ Plan B — When Even the Card Doesn't Work

  • Convenience store ingredient labels: CU, GS25, and 7-Eleven ready-meal packaging lists allergy ingredients in small print — even without icons, the ingredient names themselves are mostly given in English too.
  • Franchises publish ingredient info: large chains like Starbucks Korea and McDonald's publish per-menu-item allergy info on their website/app. Checking the app ahead of time is safer than relying on the in-store screen.
  • Translation apps: typing your sentence into Papago or Google Translate and showing the screen as-is is the last-resort fallback that repeats throughout this whole series — app setup is in #1230 Essential apps.
