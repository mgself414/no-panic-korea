① The First Mystery the Moment You Sit Down — Where Are the Utensils

Before you've even opened the menu, the first thing foreign travelers get stuck on is utensils. There's no fork, knife, or chopsticks anywhere on the table.

  • The answer is the drawer built into the side of the table. At most Korean restaurants, there's a thin drawer under the tabletop, either beside your seat or facing you, holding sujeo (수저 — a spoon-and-chopsticks set), wet wipes, and toothpicks.
  • If you don't see a drawer, check near the table legs or the side facing the next table — the handle is small and easy to miss.
  • At restaurants with no drawer at all (upscale restaurants, some café-style places), staff set your utensils for you, or you grab them yourself from a utensil bin next to the counter — if you spot a bin near the entrance, that's it.
  • If you still can't find it, don't worry. Call staff using the methods in ② below and just say "Sujeo (수저)" — they'll bring you a set right away.

② How to Call Staff — 3 Ways to Get Their Attention

Korean restaurants run on a you-call-first, don't-wait culture. It's not Western-style service where a waiter regularly circles the room making eye contact — instead, staff come when you call for them. Knowing that "staff not coming over" isn't being ignored, just how the system works, takes the pressure off.

Type How to Tell How to Call
A. Table call bell A small button (bell-shaped, labeled "hochul/호출") on the table corner or wall Press the button once → a 「Ne! (네!)」 voice or a chime at the counter → staff arrive shortly after. No need to mash it repeatedly
B. No-bell restaurant No button anywhere on the table — common at independent restaurants, bunsik (분식) shops, and traditional Korean eateries Look toward staff and say "Jeogiyo (저기요)" a bit louder than you normally would. It's not rude — this is the standard way to call staff
C. Table-order venue A tablet is placed on the table Tap the Call Staff (직원 호출) button on screen — additional orders also go through the same tablet

If you press the bell and no one comes, the sound may not have reached the counter. Wait 15–20 seconds, and if still no one shows up, try again with "Jeogiyo (저기요)".

③ Unlimited Side-Dish Refills — Why It's Free and How to Ask

Side dishes (mitbanchan, 밑반찬 — small plates like kimchi, namul, bean sprouts) are already included in the menu price, and giving you more once you finish them is the default. There's no need to feel awkward about it — that's just how the culture works.

  • Side dishes almost never show up as a separate line item on the receipt. You can drop any worry about "will a refill cost extra."
  • There are two ways to ask.
  • At restaurants with a bell: press the ② call bell, and once staff arrive, say "Banchan ribil dwaeyo? (반찬 리필 돼요?) — Can I get a side-dish refill?" or just point at the empty plate and say "Igeo deo juseyo (이거 더 주세요) — More of this, please."
  • At restaurants with a self-serve corner: if there's a separate bin or refrigerated case of side dishes against the wall, you just go help yourself to as much as you want — you don't even need to press the bell.
  • If you're not sure which type it is, just watch whether other Korean diners are walking over to a self-serve corner — that tells you instantly.

④ Water Is Self-Serve — How to Find the Water Dispenser

Some Korean restaurants have staff pour your water, but at far more places, a self-serve water dispenser is the default.

  • It's usually near the entrance wall or along the corridor to the restroom — a stainless-steel machine with cold- and hot-water buttons.
  • Grab a cup from the cup steriliser next to the dispenser (a small cabinet, you can see a UV lamp inside) — just pull the door open and take one out. You don't need to put a used cup back — return it to the dish-return station instead.
  • If a water bottle and cups are already set on your table, that restaurant is staff-serve — meaning you don't need to hunt for a self-serve dispenser.
  • If you can't tell whether it's self-serve or staff-serve, watch whether other diners get up to fetch their own water — that settles it instantly.

⑤ Napkins, Wet Wipes, and Personal Plates

  • Napkins: stand upright in a dispenser on the table, in thin tissue form. Since they're restaurant-issue napkins, they're smaller and thinner than a Western cloth napkin.
  • Wet wipes: often individually packaged inside the ① utensil drawer. If there aren't any, you can ring the bell and ask.
  • Apjeopsi (앞접시, personal plate): a small plate for when several people are sharing one dish. If it's not already set out, ask with "Apjeopsi juseyo (앞접시 주세요) — A personal plate, please" — especially essential when sharing jjigae (찌개) or jeongol (전골) stews.

Tip: no need to memorize all 12. Jeogiyo (calling staff) · Banchan ribil dwaeyo? (side dishes) · Sujeo (when you can't find the drawer) — these three cover almost every situation during a meal.

⑦ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. You can't find utensils — check the ① drawer again, and if it's still not there, call staff using ② and just say "Sujeo (수저)." No one thinks it's odd — it's a common request.

2. You pressed the bell and no one comes — there's a good chance the sound just didn't carry. After 15–20 seconds, try again with "Jeogiyo (저기요)." It helps to think of the bell and calling out loud as a two-layer backup where either one is allowed to fail.

3. You want more side dishes but feel awkward asking — there's no reason to feel awkward at all. Side-dish refills are a built-in part of how Korean restaurants work, and from the staff's side it's a routine request. One line from ③ settles it.

⑧ Plan B — Even If Words Don't Get Through

  • Gestures + pointing at the item: since utensils, water, and side dishes are all physical things right in front of you, simply pointing at an empty plate or the drawer usually gets the message across — it's a universal language.
  • Show a translation app screen: type "Could I get more side dishes?" or "Where are the spoons?" into Papago or Google Translate, let it render in Korean, and show the screen — done. App setup is covered in #1230, Essential Apps.
  • Watch the next table: if you're unsure where the self-serve water dispenser or side-dish corner is, watching where other diners walk is the fastest way to figure it out.

📌 Note — the one-dish-per-person custom: At most restaurants, ordering one dish per person is the default custom (same principle as one drink per person at cafes — #1288). To share, order as many dishes as there are people and ask for extra plates. For the 2-portion minimum at BBQ places, see #1290.
