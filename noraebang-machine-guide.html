Walk into a coin karaoke booth with zero Korean, and the real barrier isn't "what is noraebang" — it's "which of these 20 buttons on the remote gets my song playing." This manual condenses those 20 buttons into a single table.

② The Coin Karaoke Payment Machine Flow — From Choosing a Booth to Starting a Song

Step What You See on the Machine/Screen What to Do Trap
1 Light above the booth entrance Green light / unlit = free booth, red light = in use No free booth? Check your turn on the waiting screen
2 Payment machine (on the booth wall, or shared at the store entrance) Choose Pay-per-Song (곡수제) vs Pay-per-Time (시간제) Pay-per-song charges by the song, pay-per-time is a flat rate by the minute — the default varies by store
3 Cash slot / card reader Insert bills or coins, or insert/tap your card After paying, the screen shows your remaining song count or remaining time — that number is the whole scope of this visit
4 Remote-control standby screen inside the booth Pick up the remote and start searching for a song People often pay and then can't find the remote — it's usually on a fixed mount next to the mic

Price per song varies by store and time of day, but figure roughly a few hundred won. Pay-per-time is also structured as small charges in 10-minute increments, so it's not a heavy cost either way.

③ Remote-Control Button Reference Table — 15 Survival Words

Button layouts differ slightly by brand, but names and functions are nearly identical everywhere. The table below covers every Korean word printed on the remote.

Romanization (Korean) Meaning = Function
sijak (시작) Start — plays immediately once you've entered a song number
yeyak (예약) Reserve — adds the song to the back of the queue (plays next after the current song ends)
useon yeyak (우선예약) Priority reserve — cuts to the front of the queue
chwiso (취소) Cancel — cancels only the most recent reservation; the song currently playing isn't interrupted
jemok (제목) Title search — search by song title
gasu (가수) Artist search — search by artist name
han/yeong (한/영) KOR/ENG keyboard toggle — required before searching for an English song; skip it and the keyboard shows Korean characters only
beonho (번호) Song number — the number pad for direct song-number entry
sakje (삭제) Delete — erases a typo or a wrong character you entered
ganju jump (간주점프) Interlude skip — skips the intro/interlude (the instrumental-only stretch)
eko (에코) Echo — adjusts mic reverb strength, +/-
ki (키) Key — adjusts pitch by semitone (to match male/female vocal range)
tempo (템포) Tempo — adjusts playback speed
volleum (볼륨) Volume — mic or instrumental volume
banju (반주) Instrumental — plays the backing track with no vocals (practice mode)

Tip: you don't need to memorize all 15. han/yeong (한/영) — your gateway to English songs, yeyak (예약) — adding to the queue, chwiso (취소) — cancels only the last reservation. Just these three are the essentials.

④ The Master Flow for Searching English Songs — The Heart of This Piece

Skip this order and you will never find an English song. Press Title (제목) without first pressing Kor/Eng toggle (한/영), and the keyboard is in Korean — there's no way to even type English.

1. From the remote's standby screen, press Kor/Eng toggle (한/영) first — check whether an EN/ENG indicator appears in a corner of the screen

2. Press Title (제목) (or Artist (가수)) → enters search mode

3. An English QWERTY keyboard appears on screen → type just a few letters of the beginning of the song title (e.g., "SHAPE OF")

4. A results list appears, with a song number next to each song

5. Confirm the song number of the one you want → press Reserve (예약) (adds to the queue) or Start (시작) (plays immediately)

  • Trap: search without pressing Kor/Eng toggle (한/영) and the keyboard is in Korean Hangul characters, blocking English entry entirely — or if you force it, you'll get zero results. It's not "there's no English songs" — nine times out of ten, you just didn't press the toggle.
  • Tip: entering the first 3–4 letters of the title works better than the artist's name (searching the full title with special characters included often fails to match).

⑤ Entering a Song Number Directly — The Shortest Path If You Already Know It

If you looked up a song number ahead of time on Melon or YouTube, or you already found it via search, this is the fastest route.

1. Enter the song number (usually 4–6 digits) on the remote's number pad

2. Start (시작) — plays immediately if nothing's currently playing / Reserve (예약) — adds to the queue

3. For a typo, Delete (삭제) and re-enter; to start completely over, Cancel (취소)

⑥ Adjusting Echo, Key, and Tempo — Matching Your Voice and Range

These are the adjustment buttons on the remote or a wall panel inside the booth. They change in real time, even while a song is playing.

Adjustment Button Purpose
Echo Echo (에코) +/- Mic reverb strength — too much and pronunciation blurs
Key Key (키) ↑/↓ Raises or lowers pitch by semitone — for when the original key doesn't fit (male/female vocal range differences)
Tempo Tempo (템포) +/- Playback speed — nudge it down slightly if a rap section is too fast

⑦ Adding Time or Songs — When You Run Out

  • Pay-per-time: a countdown of remaining time appears on screen as it nears the end. Many stores let you add time at the payment machine even while a song is playing — no need to rush to wrap up, just head to the payment machine at your leisure.
  • Pay-per-song: once your paid songs run out, a notice appears on screen and no more reservations go through → buy more songs at the payment machine, then come back.
  • Prices vary by store and time of day, but figure roughly a few hundred won per song.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens, and How to Handle It

1. Searching turns up no English songs — 90% of the time, the cause is not pressing Kor/Eng toggle (한/영). Go back to ④ and redo it from step one.

2. You want to cancel only the reservation you just made, without stopping the song — Cancel (취소) only removes the most recent single reservation, and the song currently playing keeps going. That said, many machines can't cancel a song that's already in the middle of the queue — so double-checking the song number before you reserve is faster than having to re-enter it.

3. Your time is about to run out — when the countdown appears on screen, don't panic — just go to the payment machine and add more time while the song keeps playing, and it won't cut off. At stores where the payment machine is outside the booth, you can handle it right at the door.

⑨ Plan B — When the Remote Won't Cooperate or Things Get Confusing

  • Calling staff: most coin karaoke places are unmanned, but some have a counter or intercom. Use the intercom button, or go to the counter at the store entrance.
  • Difference from regular karaoke: coin karaoke = unmanned, small-scale, a cheap self-serve style; regular karaoke rooms — ilban noraebang (일반 노래방), also called garaoke (가라오케) — have staff on-site who set up the room for you and can be called immediately if there's trouble. That said, the remote-control operation itself is nearly identical either way, so this table applies just the same at both.
  • If nothing else works: remember that knowing just the song number (⑤) gets you straight to playback without searching — the last-resort workaround.
