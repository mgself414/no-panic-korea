🎯 What This Piece Covers — And What It Doesn't

This piece doesn't cover shop recommendations or how to pick colors and designs. It covers only "exactly what steps you go through from walking into a shop to walking out, and on what basis you pay how much deposit." In particular, a lot of people worry about whether it's normal to hand over their passport — this piece tackles that head-on.

① Why Rent a Hanbok — the Free Palace Admission Cheat Code

The biggest reason to rent a hanbok isn't photos — it's the admission fee. Wear a hanbok to the four major palaces (Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, etc.) and admission is waived — the real value isn't saving a few thousand won, it's cutting the route short by never having to stand in a ticket line at all. Around the main gate of Gyeongbokgung (Gwanghwamun), dozens of hanbok rental shops cluster within a 1-to-5-minute walk, so you can naturally swing by one on your way from your accommodation.

② Pricing System — Split Into 3 Tiers

Tier Price Range Rental Time Included
Simple ₩15,000–20,000 2–4 hours Basic jeogori (top) and chima (skirt)
Premium ₩25,000–35,000 Half-day Higher-grade fabric and embroidery, hair accessories
All-day ₩30,000–50,000 Full day Same as above + more slack on the return deadline
  • Most shops include a bag and hair accessories (dwikkoji, binyeo) in the price — if a shop asks for a separate accessory fee, it's worth comparing with another shop.
  • Couple and family set discounts vary by shop and aren't covered here — only confirmed price ranges are listed.

③ Rental Master Flow — From Walking In to Walking Out

Order What Happens Note
1 Enter the shop Gauge the shop's level from the photos and actual hanbok displayed by the door
2 Pick a design Choose by seeing the color and pattern in person — check size availability on the spot
3 Get measured Staff check length and fit, then you change in the fitting room
4 Deposit fork See ④ — hand over your passport vs. pay cash
5 Finish dressing Secure the skirt (wear the underskirt, then tie the chima strings), add hair accessories
6 Confirm the return time Make sure to note it down or photograph the return deadline staff tell you

These 6 steps stay almost identical no matter which shop you use. Step 4 is where people get confused most.

④ The Deposit Fork — Hand Over Your Passport, or Pay Cash

Korean hanbok rental shops use one of two deposit methods.

Method Condition On Return
Passport deposit 1 passport per group + ₩10,000 Passport returned immediately for a normal return
Cash deposit No passport handed over, instead pay ₩50,000 cash Full refund for a normal return

If you'd rather not hand over your passport, just bring an extra ₩50,000 in cash — this isn't a negotiation, it's an official option the shop already offers.

⑤ "Is It Okay to Hand Over My Passport?" — If You're Uneasy, Cash Is the Answer

This is where first-time international travelers hesitate most often. The short answer: the passport deposit at hanbok rental shops is standard industry practice, and it's returned immediately on your return. But if you're still uneasy, there's no need to force yourself to hand it over — switch to the cash deposit (₩50,000) from ④ and your passport stays in your own hands. Taking a photo of your passport ahead of time makes either choice feel easier.

⑥ The Free-Admission Route — Skip the Ticket Booth Entirely

Show up at a palace wearing a hanbok and you don't need to stand in the ticket booth line at all. Go straight to the main gate's ticket-check point (entrance gate) and show staff you're wearing a hanbok, and you're through — the whole step of buying a ticket or scanning a QR code is skipped. It's common for confused visitors to stand in the ticket booth line and only get redirected later, so finding the ticket-check gate signage first is the time-saving move.

⑦ The Closing-Time Trap — Last Rental and Last Return Are Different Deadlines

  • Some shops cut off last rental intake at 3:00 PM — show up in the late afternoon and you might not be able to rent at all that day.
  • Even the all-day pass isn't unlimited — the standard return deadline is usually 6:00 PM. Trust the name "all-day" alone, come back late, and you're counted late.
  • The safe move is to ask both today's last-rental time and the return deadline the moment you walk into the shop. In winter, some shops move the deadline earlier because daylight is shorter.

⑧ Return Inspection — Normal Return vs. Late

On return, staff do a quick check of the garment's condition (stains, damage) and then give back the deposit (passport or cash). If there's no problem, it's over in 1–2 minutes.

If you're late: most shops handle it by charging an extra fee proportional to how long you overran, and outright forfeiting the deposit is rare. Still, if you think you'll be late, the safest move is to let the shop know ahead of time, by phone or in person, before the return deadline.

⑩ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. No cash for the deposit — ask first whether card payment works; if not, withdraw cash at a nearby convenience-store ATM (usually within a 5-minute walk). ATMs are common around Gyeongbokgung since it's a tourist commercial area.

2. You think you'll be late for the return — don't panic and just show up late; contact or visit the shop before the deadline to let them know your situation. It's an extra fee in most cases, nothing more.

3. The skirt is slipping down or coming loose — the underskirt strings or the chima waist ties have loosened. Walk into any shop and ask for help, or if the shop you rented from is nearby, go back and they'll refix it for free.

⑪ Plan B — When the Rental Shop Is Full or the Situation Is Unclear

  • If the shop is full: shops cluster within a 1-to-5-minute walk of each other in front of Gyeongbokgung, so moving to the next shop over solves it most of the time.
  • If the size doesn't fit: don't force it on — ask staff for different stock. If free admission is your goal, the standard is "it visibly looks like a hanbok," not a perfect fit, so there's no need to obsess over it.
  • If your payment card doesn't go through: see the card-decline response in the #1275 payment and tipping guide.
