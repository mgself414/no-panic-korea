🎯 How to Read This Manual — Notation Conventions

If other pieces in this series cover "after you land in Korea," this one comes before that. It gathers only what to finish before departure so you don't end up standing in another line at the airport. Each item links out to a more detailed piece.

  • A button on a screen goes in brackets: Apply
  • A document or screen title goes in guillemets: 「e-Arrival Card」
  • A hands-on action is bold: take a screenshot and save it offline
  • A sentence to show an officer or staff member goes in code: 숙소 주소입니다 (sukso jusoimnida)

⚠ Notation Principle: The systems and limits below are official government information as of September 2026. Entry rules change every year, so check the official site again right before departure. Items with no official guidance, like app install tips, are labeled "Tip."

① D-30 — Sort Out Your Documents

K-ETA (Electronic Travel Authorization)

  • The temporary exemption for most visa-free entry countries runs from January 1 through December 31, 2026. If you're exempt, you don't need to apply.
  • Even if you're exempt, you can still apply voluntarily (a fee of KRW 10,000, valid for 3 years or until your passport expires).
  • If you do apply, the recommendation is at least 72 hours before boarding. Approval usually comes within 72 hours.
  • Not eligible to apply at all: holders of diplomatic/official passports, ABTC holders, flight/ship crew, transit passengers, and those 17 or under or 65 or over.

Passport

  • Check your remaining validity. Make sure it matches the spelling of your name in English on your ticket — this is exactly what trips people up at ticketing and ID checks (#1394).

② D-7 — The e-Arrival Card and Booking Checks

e-Arrival Card

  • Starting in 2026, the paper arrival card is gone, unified into the electronic form.
  • File it free on the official site (e-arrivalcard.go.kr) starting 3 days before arrival.
  • Exempt: people who actually hold an issued K-ETA, and holders of an Alien Registration Card, Permanent Residency Card, or Domestic Residence Report Card.
  • Put the other way around, most nationals of K-ETA exemption countries still have to file this. "I'm from an exempt country, so I don't need to do anything" is wrong.
  • When filling it out, enter your stay address (cheryuji / 체류지) — your accommodation — and a contact number. If you're staying at multiple places, use your first stay or the one you'll stay at longest.

The step-by-step screen flow is in #1289.

③ D-3 — What Not to Pack

Item Rule
Fresh meat (pork, beef, chicken, etc.) Banned from import
Processed meat (ham, sausage, jerky, etc.) Mostly banned
Caught not declaring Fine up to KRW 10 million
Personal medication Up to 6 bottles, or a 3-month supply per the dosage
Health supplements Up to 6 bottles
Medication containing narcotic substances Separate approval required

Work out your duty-free limits ahead of time too.

Category Limit
Personal effects overall USD 800 or less
Alcohol Total 2L, USD 400 or less
Cigarettes 200 sticks
E-cigarette nicotine liquid 20ml
Perfume 100ml

If you're under 19, the alcohol and tobacco duty-free allowance doesn't apply to you. If you're on medication, it's safer to bring your prescription and an English-language doctor's note too (#1352).

④ D-1 — Five Screens to Screenshot in Advance

In case your internet cuts out mid-trip or your battery runs low, take screenshots and pin them to the front of your photo album. This is also the reason this piece exists.

1. Your accommodation's address in Korean — for showing straight to a taxi driver. An English-only address sometimes doesn't work.

2. Your accommodation booking confirmation — check that it matches the stay address you wrote on the arrival card.

3. Your return flight details — flight number, date, and time.

4. The e-Arrival Card submission confirmation screen.

5. The photo page of your passport — this speeds up reissue if you lose it (#1392).

Tip — Keep a copy of your passport photo page in the cloud too, but not as a shared link.

⑤ Good to Install Before You Leave (Tips)

This section is practical tips, not official guidance. Still, the reasoning is clear.

  • Maps: In Korea, Google Maps' walking and transit directions aren't accurate. Naver Map and Kakao Map are the de facto standard (#1295).
  • Naver account: Since June 2026, passport verification has been open, so you can book, order, and pay through Naver Map without a Korean phone number. Setting one up in advance saves time once you're there (#1394).
  • Transit: Kakao T lets you sign up with a foreign number and a foreign card (#1273).
  • eSIM: Install it before departure, but activate it only after you land. Get this order wrong and your data runs out early (#1303).
  • The full list of essential apps is in #1230.

⚠ 3 Common Mistakes

1. "I'm from a K-ETA exempt country, so I don't need to do anything" — If you didn't actually get a K-ETA, you still have to file the e-Arrival Card. The two exemptions apply to different things.

2. Packing jerky or sausage as souvenirs — processed meat is mostly banned too. If you're caught not declaring it, the fine is up to KRW 10 million.

3. Saving your accommodation address only in English — it may not work in a taxi. Screenshot the Korean-language address separately.

⚙ 3 FAQs

1. Which do I file, K-ETA or the e-Arrival Card? — If you got a K-ETA, you're exempt from the arrival card. If you're from a temporarily exempt country and didn't get a K-ETA, you file the arrival card.

2. From when can I file it? — Starting 3 days before arrival. Too early doesn't work.

3. Can I bring a lot of medication? — For personal use, up to 6 bottles or a 3-month supply. Beyond that, or if it contains narcotic substances, you need a separate procedure (#1352).
