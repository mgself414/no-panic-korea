① The Entry Master Flow — From the Door to Your Seat

Step Situation What to Do Trap
1 Walk in and check for an open seat Claim one first if there's a seat Ordering before checking, then standing to drink because there's no seat left, is the most common panic
2 Save your seat Leave a coat, book, or bag (nothing valuable) on the chair or table Don't leave a wallet, phone, or laptop — Korea being safe doesn't mean a traveler should make the exception
3 Order at the counter or kiosk (#1270) Answer "For here or to go?" with "Maejang-iyo" (매장이요) or "Pojang-iyo" (포장이요) Your answer decides your cup (see ②)
4 Buzzer and pickup (#1288) Head to the pickup counter when it buzzes Take the whole tray back to your seat
5 While you stay One drink per person; check the outlet and time signage (③) A shop with its own posted rules takes priority over this manual
6 Leaving Sort your tray and cup at Return Station (④, #1276) Walking out and leaving it behind gets noticed

② Cups — Why You Get a Mug

  • Disposable plastic cups [Rule] — Drink inside and a shop legally can't serve you in a disposable plastic cup (Resource Recycling Act enforcement rules). So say "maejang-iyo" and you get a mug or glass. A violation puts the shop on the hook for a fine, not the customer.
  • Paper cups [changing] — Regulated in 2022 → excluded from regulation (voluntary) in November 2023 → reregulation under review as of August 2026 (no start date set). So some shops hand out paper cups and some don't.
  • Need to leave — Drinking from a mug but heading out? Tell the counter: "Pojang-euro bakkwo juseyo" (포장으로 바꿔 주세요) — "please switch this to takeout." They'll transfer it into a disposable cup.
  • Your own tumbler [Optional] — Hand one over and most shops will use it; some brands even give a discount (amount varies by shop — check in-store).
  • Ice water and straws — At the self-serve corner (#1288). More shops now use paper or rice straws.

③ Seat, Time, and Outlets — the Signage Has the Answer

Signage Meaning Tag
"One drink per person" One drink per person — don't sit three to a table on a single drink [Custom]
"2-hour time limit" / "Please limit kagong (laptop) use" Long-stay limit in effect [Custom]
"Outlets available" / "Please limit laptop use" Whether the shop has outlets [Custom]
"No kids allowed" Children can't enter with you [Custom]
"Pets welcome" No sign means, as a rule, no pets [Custom]
  • Saving a seat [Custom] — Marking a seat with a coat or book in a Korean cafe is normal, and sitting in a seat someone else has claimed is rude. No seat open? Stand and wait or try another shop — don't ask to share a table.
  • Belongings [Optional] — Plenty of locals leave a laptop behind to use the restroom, but travelers should keep their wallet and passport on them.
  • Long stays [Optional] — A shop with no posted sign is usually lenient. Packing up and leaving once it starts filling up is the local's sense of timing.

④ Leaving — 30 Seconds at the Return Station

1. Take the whole tray to Return Station (by the entrance or counter).

2. Pour out ice and leftover drink into the Ice / Drink bin.

3. Sort your cup into Plastic or Paper; mugs and glasses go into Reusable Cup.

4. Straws, lids, and napkins go in General Trash (some shops put straws and lids in the plastic slot instead).

5. Stack the tray on the tray shelf. Full sorting detail is in #1276.

No need to wipe the table. Bringing a mug back to the counter yourself is a nice touch, but the return station is enough.

⑥ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. You got a mug and now you have to leave soon — At the counter, say "Pojang-euro bakkwo juseyo" (포장으로 바꿔 주세요). They'll transfer it over. Say "pojang-iyo" from the start if you already know you're leaving.

2. You saved a seat and someone's sitting in it when you get back — If you left a coat, it's usually a mistake. One line — "Yeogi je jariyeyo" (여기 제 자리예요) — "this is my seat" — and they'll move. If you didn't leave anything, it wasn't a claimed seat.

3. You opened your laptop at an outlet seat and staff says something — It's a "please limit laptop use" shop. Move or use it briefly and go. Not worth arguing about.

⑦ Plan B — When You're Not Sure

  • Not sure if you can take a seat? Ask staff: "Jari isseoyo?" (자리 있어요?). No seat, try another shop.
  • Not sure which cup you'll get? Say "maejang-iyo" — a mug is the default, and you can switch when you leave.
  • No return station in sight? Hand the tray back at the counter.
