  • Buttons to press or spots to place things appear in brackets: Buzzer Return (진동벨 반납대)
  • Actions you do by hand are bold: when it buzzes, pick up the buzzer and head to the counter

This piece has exactly one angle. Not which cafe to go to — only what to do once you've ordered and paid.

① Korean Cafes Are Self-Only — Don't Wait for Table Service

Korean cafes — Starbucks, A Twosome Place, Ediya, Mega Coffee, nearly all of them — run on a self-service structure with no table service. Ordering, paying, picking up, and returning your tray are all on you; staff bringing your drink to your table is, in principle, not a thing. So no matter how long you sit and wait, no one is coming — it's neither broken nor being ignored. Just knowing this is the basic structure makes the procedure in ② below click into place naturally.

The ordering screen itself (kiosk vs. counter, where the pay button is) is covered in #1270, the kiosk-ordering guide. This piece starts from the moment payment is done.

② The Post-Order Master Flow — Pseudo-Screens

Even across different brands, these 6 steps never change.

Step Situation What to Do Trap
1 Pay first at the counter/kiosk Payment locks in your drink order at the same time Prepay is the default at Korean cafes — it's not a "get your drink, pay later" structure
2 Staff hands you a buzzer, saying 「jindongbel O-beon-imnida (진동벨 O번입니다) — You're buzzer number O」 Take the buzzer and head to a seat The buzzer is on loan from the cafe — you must return it at the end
3 Claim a seat you like Mark it with a bag, jacket, etc. Seating is first-come, first-served — there's no reservation concept
4 Drink is ready → the buzzer vibrates and flashes Take the buzzer and head to the counter At many cafes the vibration only lasts a few seconds — check the buzzer occasionally while waiting
5 At the counter, place the buzzer at Buzzer Return (진동벨 반납대) and pick up your drink Take the tray and return to your seat Walking off without returning the buzzer leaves the cafe short of buzzers for the next customer
6 After finishing your drink, return your tray yourself Process it at the return station in order (see ⑥) Don't just dump it all in — details in the link below

The core is that "go get it, give it back, clean up after yourself" — all three actions — are the customer's job. Once these three actions are second nature, they work identically at any cafe.

③ If There's No Buzzer — the Nickname/Order-Number Call Branch

Some cafes, Starbucks especially, use a call name instead of a buzzer. Register a nickname when you order, and staff calls it out — 「OOO gogaeknim (OOO 고객님) — Customer OOO」 — when your drink is ready. No buzzer to wait for, but you do need to listen for your own name.

  • Call-name cafes: remember the nickname you registered and listen for it near the counter. It's easy to miss over the music, so don't claim a seat too far away.
  • Small cafes with neither a buzzer nor call names: they call an order number instead — 「OOO-beon gogaeknim (OOO번 고객님) — Customer number OOO」 — or match the number printed on your receipt against the counter screen.
  • If you're ever unsure, the surest fix is to hold your receipt in hand and match it against whatever name or number gets called at the counter.

④ The Self-Serve Corner — How to Use the Water, Straws, Napkins, and Syrup

There's a self-serve bar (self-serve corner) off to the side of the pickup counter or elsewhere in the cafe. You help yourself — no need to ask staff.

  • Water dispenser: if the self-serve bar has one, it's free. Cups are usually provided, or you can use your own.
  • Straws and napkins: kept in containers at the self-serve bar. Take as many as you need.
  • Syrup, sugar, cinnamon powder: depending on the brand, some self-serve bars have syrup pumps — if there's one, add freely.
  • Can't find the self-serve bar? Check near the counter, or ask "ppaldae eodi isseoyo? (빨대 어디 있어요?)" — "where are the straws?" — and staff will point you right to it.

⑤ One Drink Per Person, Outlets, and Long Stays — Cafe Culture in One Line

One drink per person is the basic manner at Korean cafes, and "kagong (카공, cafe studying)" — sitting for hours with a laptop open — is common enough that many seats come with power outlets. The longer you occupy a seat, the more it's expected that you'll order again to keep up the manners — the etiquette around outlets and seating is covered in more detail in #1279 Outlets & Voltage Guide §⑤.

⑥ After You Finish — the Tray-Return Sequence

Don't just dump the tray, cup, and straw into the return station as-is. Process them empty → sort → deposit, in that order — empty out ice and leftover liquid first, then separate the straw and lid from the cup. The full procedure, plus what to do when there's no return station, is laid out as pseudo-screens in #1276 Trash & Cup Return Guide — check that one too.

pikeop (픽업) Pickup

maejang (매장) Eat in

kolneim (콜네임) Call name — being called by a registered nickname

Tip: of the 12, remembering just three gets you through the whole procedure — jindongbel/buzzer (waiting) · pikeop/pickup (collecting) · bannap/return (wrapping up).

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens and How to Handle It

1. The buzzer hasn't gone off in a while — at a busy cafe, it can take 15–20 minutes. Don't waste time suspecting the battery or the vibration sensor — if it's taking a long time, take your receipt to the counter and ask "je jumun nawannayo? (제 주문 나왔나요?)" — "has my order come out?"

2. You missed the call — some cafes only call a call-name or number once and move on. If your drink hasn't shown up, go to the counter, show your receipt, and ask them to check — they'll get it sorted right away.

3. You've claimed a seat but worry about leaving it too long — marking a seat with a bag or jacket while you step out to the restroom or the counter is common practice in Korea. A brief absence isn't an issue.

⑨ Plan B — One Sentence for When the Procedure Gets Confusing

  • If you weren't given a buzzer, or aren't sure whether you were: "jindongbel juseyo (진동벨 주세요)" — "buzzer, please"
  • If you've been waiting and your drink seems late: "je jumun nawannayo? (제 주문 나왔나요?)" — "has my order come out?"

These two sentences resolve most delays and mix-ups on the spot at the counter.
