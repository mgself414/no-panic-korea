🎯 How to Read This Piece — Screen Notation Rules

This piece reuses the notation rules set in #1270 as-is.

  • Buttons on a screen appear in brackets: Pay (결제하기)
  • Screen titles and prompts appear in corner brackets: 「chulbal-yeok geomsaek (출발역 검색) — Search departure station」
  • Actions you do by hand are bold: insert your card all the way in

This piece isn't about how to book a ticket — it fills the procedural gap between arriving at the station and sitting down on the train. For an intro to KTX itself (fares, seat classes, comparison with other transit), see #239 first.

① 3 Booking Routes — Where and How to Buy

Method Where Foreign Cards Notes
Korail Talk app App Store / Google Play ★ Goes through far more reliably than the website Booking opens 30 days before departure, choose your own seat
Official website (letskorail.com) PC/mobile browser Rejected frequently (a VAN infrastructure quirk) Backup only, if you haven't installed the app
Station ticket machine Lobby of major stations like Seoul Station Machines that support foreign cards exist separately (check for card-network logos) Instant on-site issuing, has a language toggle button

Korea's payment network only partially supports the international EMV standard, so foreign cards are frequently rejected on the website (see #527 for the structure). The Korail Talk app is your top-priority route — install it before you leave (see #1230, the essential apps piece).

② The Korail Talk Booking Master Flow — Screen by Screen

1. 「chulbal-yeok (출발역) — Departure station」 search box → type the station name in English (e.g. Seoul) → tap it from the list

2. 「dochak-yeok (도착역) — Arrival station」 → search in English the same way

3. Pick a date and time from the calendar → Search (조회)

4. Train list (KTX/Mugunghwa/SRT are shown mixed together — confirm it says KTX) → Select (선택) your preferred time

5. Touch your seat directly on the seat map (window/aisle) → Next (다음)

6. Payment screen → enter your card details → Pay (결제하기)

Once booking is complete, the app screen itself is your ticket. No need to print anything — but deleting the app or logging out means losing your ticket, so leave it alone until the train arrives.

③ "There's No Ticket Gate" — the Biggest Culture Shock

Unlike the subway (#1281), there's no ticket gate at the entrance to a KTX platform. Physically, you can just walk in without a ticket. This isn't a loophole — it's an honor-system, gateless design, built on the assumption that every passenger is carrying a valid ticket.

  • A conductor periodically walks through the train to check tickets
  • A screenshot doesn't count as proof — only the actual booking screen, logged into the Korail Talk app, is valid
  • Getting caught riding without a valid ticket brings a fine (a multiple of the fare)

Don't relax just because "I don't see anyone checking" — keep your ticket accessible in the app at all times.

④ Reading the Display Board — Finding Your Platform (Taneun Got, 타는 곳)

The display board in the station concourse shows 「yeolcha beonho (열차번호) · destination · departure time · taneun got (타는 곳)」. The key trap: the platform number is only revealed about 15 minutes before departure. It's normal for the field to be blank until then.

Train No. Destination Departure Platform
KTX 401 Busan 09:15 7
KTX 105 Daejeon 09:20 — (not yet revealed)

Match the train number from your booking screen to the row on the display board. If several trains have similar destinations and times, the train number is the one unique identifier.

⑤ Getting a Sense of Seoul Station's Scale — When and Where to Move

Seoul Station's KTX platforms run from No. 3 to No. 14, spread out widely underground. Arriving 20–30 minutes before departure is recommended — there's no gate to slow you down, but the station is large enough that walking from the concourse to checking the board to the escalators/stairs takes real time.

Route: enter the concourse → check the platform number on the display board (15 min before) → head down the stairs/escalator toward that number → platform.

⑥ Finding Your Seat — Car Number, Seat Number, and Rear-Facing Seats

The car number (hocha, 호차) is displayed next to each train door. Match it against your booking screen and board through the correct door. Seats are labeled with a number (row) plus a letter (left/right). One thing to note: some seats face backward, against the direction of travel — if there's no direction-selection option at booking, you may be randomly assigned one. If that's uncomfortable, change your seat at the ticket window or in the app's reselection screen before boarding.

⑦ On-Board Manners, in a Paragraph

Take phone calls quietly in the connecting space between cars. Bringing in and eating food (bento boxes, etc.) is allowed — cart service is common too. There's a dedicated large-suitcase rack near the doors — leaving a bag in the seat-side aisle blocks other passengers from moving.

⑨ 3 Panic Scenarios

1. The platform isn't showing on the display board — this panics people who don't know the 15-minutes-before rule. Fix: wait near the board in the concourse until the 15-minute mark.

2. You missed your train — KTX is reserved-seating, so you don't get automatically carried over to the next train. Go to the ticket window or the 「seungchagwon gwanli (승차권 관리) — Ticket management」 menu in Korail Talk to change or refund it.

3. You boarded the wrong car — since there's no gate check, nobody stops you on the spot. Just walk through to the correct car and seat before the next stop.

⑩ Plan B — If You're Still Stuck

Showing your Korail Talk booking screen to a station staff member resolves most situations. If you're touring multiple cities repeatedly, the foreigner-only Korail Pass is also worth considering.

📌 Note — SRT: The high-speed SRT departing from Suseo Station is run by a separate company — book it on the SRT app/website, not Korail Talk. If you're staying in the Gangnam area, Suseo may be closer, so check which station your train leaves from before booking.
