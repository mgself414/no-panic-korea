① What Is the e-Arrival Card — the Electronic Version of the Paper Arrival Card

Starting January 1, 2026, the e-Arrival Card became mandatory, officially replacing the paper arrival card that used to be handed out on the plane with an electronic declaration system. The procedure itself is simple — before you land, you fill in your passport, flight, and address-of-stay details on a website, get a QR code, and show that QR code at immigration when you arrive. That's it. That said, because the system is still in its early transition, some flights and airports are reportedly still handing out paper arrival cards. If you get a paper card on board anyway, don't panic — just fill it out and submit it as the flight crew instructs, and it'll be processed as normal. If you've already submitted online, treat the paper card as a backup and just follow the instructions at immigration.

② e-Arrival vs. K-ETA — Sorting Out the Two Most Confused Terms

This is where most people get confused. The two are completely separate systems, and depending on your situation, you may need both.

Category K-ETA e-Arrival Card
What it is Pre-travel authorization — approval you get before boarding the plane Arrival declaration — replaces the paper form you'd fill out on entry
Timing Before departure (well ahead, right after booking your flight) Within 72 hours of arrival
Where K-ETA official website/app www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr
If you skip it You may be denied boarding entirely You may need a separate manual procedure at the airport
Applicable countries Exemption/requirement status keeps changing by country In principle, applies to all short-term foreign visitors
How to check Whether K-ETA applies to your nationality → check the official site Whether you need to declare → check the portal

The core takeaway: K-ETA is pre-approval for "can I board?" — e-Arrival is a declaration that "I've arrived." They come in a different order and live on different websites. Always confirm separately, through official channels, whether K-ETA applies to your nationality.

③ The Portal Submission Master Flow — Screen by Screen

There's only one official portal: www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr. Type it directly into your browser's address bar — don't click sponsored search results or third-party agency sites.

Step What You See on Screen Action to Take Trap
1 Portal home screen Select language English Double-check the address bar shows the official domain
2 「Passport Information」 Enter your passport number, nationality, and date of birth Match the spelling letter-for-letter against your passport
3 「Flight Information」 Enter your airline, flight number, and arrival date Open your e-ticket beforehand and copy it exactly
4 「Address of Stay Information」 Enter your accommodation's address in English Copy the address straight from your booking confirmation (Booking.com or a hotel email) — this is the same "I don't know my address" problem as in the #1272 delivery-app guide, so screenshot it before you depart
5 「Confirm Information」 Review everything → Submit Check for typos on this final confirmation screen before submitting
6 「Application Complete」 The screen where your QR code is issued Once you see this screen, you're done — save it per the next section

④ How to Save Your QR Code — Screenshot Plus Offline Storage

Take a screenshot on the spot of your issued QR code, and if possible, also download the captured image separately and save it to your photo album. Roaming or Wi-Fi often doesn't kick in right away when you land, so the key is making sure it opens straight from your photo album without needing the internet. If you have time, printing out the screenshot as a backup is a safe second layer.

⑤ The 72-Hour Timing Strategy — When to Fill It Out

You can submit within 72 hours (3 days) of arrival — no earlier. That said, there's no need to rush through it at the boarding gate either. Here's the practical timing.

  • About 3 days before departure, collect screenshots of your accommodation booking confirmation (the hotel's English address) and your flight details ahead of time.
  • The night before departure, while you have stable Wi-Fi at home, submit following the flow in ③ and save your QR code.
  • If you didn't get to it, doing it while waiting at the departure gate isn't too late either — just make sure you're still within 72 hours of your arrival time.

⑦ Your Route After Landing — What Actually Happens at Immigration

1. Once you're off the plane in the arrivals area, look for the 「Foreigner」 line on the signage and get in it.

2. When it's your turn, present your passport, then show the saved QR code screen (it gets scanned, or the officer checks it directly).

3. If there's a fingerprint or facial photo step, follow the officer's instructions.

4. Once the officer stamps your passport or confirms it on screen and waves you through, you're done — head to baggage claim.

You don't need to explain anything up front during this whole process. Just hand over your passport and QR code in the order they're expected.

⑧ 3 Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens and How to Handle It

1. You forgot to submit and just arrived — don't panic, just tell the immigration officer what happened. It gets handled on the spot, following their instructions. This isn't a reason to get scared and hesitate about traveling in the first place.

2. Your QR code won't open (no internet connection) — show the offline screenshot you saved to your photo album in ④. This is exactly why the screenshot is essential.

3. You got stuck because you don't know your address of stay — open the hotel booking confirmation email you prepared before departure and copy the address from there. If you can't find the email either, use the airport Wi-Fi to log into your booking app (Booking.com, etc.) and check it.

⑨ Plan B — When the Portal Gets Stuck or Confusing

  • Ask staff for help: tell an airline counter staff member or an immigration officer, "I need help with the e-Arrival Card," and they'll walk you through it. This is a new system that travelers from all over the world already run into every day, so airport staff are used to guiding people through it.
  • Team up with a travel companion: if you're traveling with someone, having one person read from the table in ③ while the other enters the information leads to the fewest mistakes.
  • As a last resort: if nothing else works, just following the on-site instructions is enough — the system isn't set up so that a stuck arrival declaration blocks you from entering the country.

📌 Note — Q-CODE (quarantine declaration): Separate from e-Arrival, travelers departing from certain designated countries/regions only may also need to file a Q-CODE health declaration (unrelated to COVID — a standing quarantine system). Most travelers are not affected. Check the official quarantine portal before departure.
