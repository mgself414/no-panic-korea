① The Number to Save After 112·119 — "When in a Bind, 1330"

If you're hurt, there's a fire, or you're the victim of a crime, call 119·112 right away. The next number to save is 1330. Most panic moments on a trip come not from danger but from the language barrier — when you're lost, when you've left something behind, when you can't make yourself understood in a shop. 1330 is the tourist interpretation and guidance window that lowers that barrier. If you remember nothing more than "119·112 for emergencies, 1330 when you're in a bind," this guide has done its job.

② What Is 1330 — Tourist Hotline Profile

Item Detail
Operated by Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) travel info & interpretation hotline
Number 1330, no area code needed
Hours Open every day of the year. Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, and Malay/Indonesian are 08:00–19:00 (hours for the other languages are not stated in the official notice)
Languages 8 languages by phone (Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Vietnamese, Thai, Malay/Indonesian)
Cost Internet voice calls made over Wi-Fi are free
Channels Phone + text chat (only 4 languages: Korean, English, Japanese, Chinese) — 1330 Messenger, KakaoTalk, Facebook, LINE, WhatsApp

③ 3 Numbers — Which One, and When

Number Handles When
1330 Tourist info, interpretation, and any kind of trouble in general If you don't know what to dial, start here no matter what
112 Police Reporting crime or accidents — dial directly
119 Fire / paramedics Fire or medical emergencies — dial directly
  • In an emergency, skip 1330 and dial 119·112 directly. Give your location first, then the situation, keep it short, and don't hang up (National Fire Agency). 119 also accepts text, photo, and video reports for foreigners and others who find it hard to report by voice (Policy Briefing).
  • 1330 is the number for non-emergency bind situations — directions, transport, lost items, communicating with a shop — where you need interpretation and guidance.

④ Getting Interpretation from 1330 — When It's Not an Emergency

1330's value is interpretation and guidance. Here's how the call goes.

1. Call 1 ] [ 3 ] [ 3 ] [ 0 → choose your language

2. Explain the situation in your language ("I left my bag in a taxi.")

3. The operator tells you whom to contact and what to say, and interprets what you need

4. If you can't communicate with shop staff or a taxi driver, hand over the phone on speakerphone so the operator can speak to them directly

We could not confirm from official notices whether 1330 can connect you straight to 112·119 in a joint call (the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism announced a plan in 2012 to strengthen links between 1330 and 112·119). So in an emergency, call 119·112 directly first.

⑥ Making the Call — Exactly as It Appears

  • Dial 1 ] [ 3 ] [ 3 ] [ 0 (no area code) then the call button
  • If you're on a Korean SIM or roaming plan, that's it. If it won't connect while on international roaming, add the country code and try again as +82-2-1330
  • If it still won't connect, don't waste more time trying — the fastest option is to ask the hotel front desk to make the call for you (see ⑩)

⑦ If a Phone Call Feels Like Too Much — Text Chat

If speaking feels intimidating, you can use 1330 text chat instead of calling. Chat is available only in Korean, English, Japanese, and Chinese. You type out the situation, so there's no worry about pronunciation or background noise. For an urgent situation, call 119·112 instead of chatting — save chat for non-urgent questions or interpretation requests.

⑨ Panic Scenarios — What Actually Happens and How to Handle It

1. You're at the ER and nothing gets through — call 1330 and hold the phone out to the front desk or medical staff on speakerphone. The operator interprets everything, from explaining symptoms to giving instructions.

2. The police showed up but there's no communication — same move: call 1330 and put it on speakerphone so the officer can listen along. The operator interprets everything from filing the report to taking your statement.

The key point in both cases: the process doesn't stall just because you don't speak Korean.

⑩ Plan B — If Even 1330 Doesn't Work Out

  • Hotel front desk: staff will often make the call for you or explain the situation directly. If your accommodation is nearby, this is the fastest option.
  • Papago translation screen: type the situation in English and show the translated screen as-is — app setup is covered in #1230 Essential apps guide.
